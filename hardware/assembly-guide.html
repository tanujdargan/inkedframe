inkedframe: assembly

Orange arrows show where each part goes; parts being added are drawn lifted above their place.

Print: board halves, joining strip, 2 plungers, USB block, stand base and leg (black); 4 mat strips, 4 keys, name plates (white). Reused: clamp, battery tray, 6 panel clips. Hardware (nylon M3): 12 × M3×6, 2 × M3×8, 2 × M3×10, 18 nuts, 2 zip ties.

1Nuts into the back

  • Board halves outside face up. Press an M3 nut into each hex pocket: 6 along the seam, 4 for the battery tray, 2 for the USB block.
  • A strip of tape over them stops them dropping out when you turn the board over.

2Join the halves

  • Turn over, inside face up. Butt the halves together at the seam.
  • Lay the joining strip over the seam and screw it down with 6 × M3×6 into the nuts.

3Plungers and inside nuts

  • Drop the plungers into their guides from the inside, flange up. Boot is the longer one (6.6 mm), the hole further from the USB end. Reset is the shorter one.
  • Slide an M3 nut into the side slot of each of the two clamp posts, and into each of the 4 stand-socket bosses (2 in the middle for landscape, 2 to the right for portrait). They can't be reached once the frame is closed.

4Feather

  • Face down (buttons towards the board), USB end at the bottom.
  • Slide the top (antenna) end under the lip, then lower the USB end onto the two pins.
  • Your printed clamp goes over the USB end: 2 × M3×8 into the post nuts, just snug.

5Battery

  • Tray upright, open end up: 4 × M3×6 from inside, heads in the tray's pockets, into the nuts in the back.
  • Battery in, wire corner on the side with the comb, push until it clicks. Wire into the comb, plug into the Feather from the right (the plug slides in through its pocket).

6USB-C port

  • Press the female end into the channel from the top (face at the open front, back end against the back wall, cable into the slot); it clicks in.
  • Pull a zip tie tight round the cable right behind the back wall, and a second through the slot under the channel and round the plug. Trim both: now it can't move in, out or up.
  • Block onto the board with 2 × M3×6 into the nuts. Plug the other end into the Feather (the plug lies in the pocket below it). Coil spare cable away from the top of the Feather (the antenna).

7Inside done

  • Wire the display driver board to the Feather and lay it in the clear area at the bottom middle.

8Mat: strips and keys

  • Lay the four white strips face down and push the corners together.
  • Press a bow-tie key into each corner pocket from the back. A drop of glue makes it permanent.

9Panel into the mat

  • Panel into the pocket, display side down, ribbon cable through the notch at the bottom.
  • Press the 6 clips onto their pegs, each reaching over the panel's edge.

10Name plate

  • Turn the mat over and drop a name plate into the recess, letters out. The acrylic holds it.
  • Stacked one for portrait; the blank one if you don't want a name. Lift it out with a piece of tape.

11Into the frame

  • Acrylic, then the mat with the panel. Connect the ribbon cable to the driver board.
  • Backboard in, inside face towards the panel, and close the frame's tabs.

12Desk stand

  • Screw the stand base onto the landscape socket (middle) or the portrait socket (left of centre, seen from the back) with 2 × M3×10 from outside.
  • Slot the leg's barrels between the base's and push the printed pin through all five. The leg opens until its tails press on the board (about 18° of lean) and folds down flat.

13Wall

  • Take the stand off. Hang on two screws: landscape uses the two top holes, portrait (frame turned clockwise) the two down the right edge as seen from the back. The top corner hole works both ways.
